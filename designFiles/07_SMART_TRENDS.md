# Smart Trends Engine (the "not-quite-AI" layer)

Guiding principle from the user: **the "smart" lives in the trend analysis, not in workout planning.** This app never sequences your workout or balances movement selection — you decide what to train. It only answers two things well: *are you recovered*, and *what could you lift*. Framed as a navigation system, not a coach.

Build in layers. Do not attempt layer 4 until layers 1-3 are solid and there's months of real logged data — a trained model will not beat well-tuned heuristics on this little data anyway.

## Layer 1 — Rule-based recency/rotation (implemented)
Simple recency queries: days since each lift was last trained (`TrendEngine.daysSinceLastTrained`), surfaced on Home status cards: "X hasn't been trained in N days — looks primed" / "X was just trained — likely still recovering." (Still text-based/unrevisited — see the compass note below.)

**Superseded:** the Lifts list's readiness bars used to be pure recency (0-5, days-since-trained). Now reads from `ReadinessEngine` — same compass as Home's muscle map — see below.

## Chart trend lines vs. the prediction box (important distinction)
The dashed trend line drawn on every chart (`LabeledTrendChart`, see `00_UX_DESIGN.md`) is **cosmetic/statistical only** — moving-average curves for weight/steps/sleep, a quadratic fit for lift strength history. It is NOT the same thing as the "Next Heavy Set" box on the Lift detail page (below), which is a distinct, bespoke calculation. Don't conflate the two when reasoning about "the prediction" — the chart's dashed line is a simple trend/extrapolation; the box is the one that reasons about your own characteristic rep range and recent history.

## Layer 2 — REWORKED (2026-07-15): dropped e1RM-range prediction and decay entirely, replaced with a same-rep-count projection

**What changed and why.** The original `TrendEngine.predictNextE1rm` box (described below in the superseded section) and the Goal gauge's decay curve (`StrengthStandards.effectiveBestE1rm`, since deleted) were both built on more machinery than the underlying science actually supports at this level. The user's own call, after using the app against her real logged data and finding a concrete miss (a deadlift prediction thrown off because Epley assumes submaximal effort at higher rep counts, which wasn't true for a near-failure `205×2` set): *"we might have gotten too excited... adding in decay and growth curves I think was a mistake at least at this level, thinking there was more hard science behind predicting 1RM than there was."* Two changes followed directly from that:
1. **No more rep-to-1RM conversion in the near-term prediction at all.** `TrendEngine.predictNextAtCharacteristicReps` replaces `predictNextE1rm` (see below).
2. **No more decay.** `StrengthStandards.effectiveBestE1rm` (the Goal gauge's grace-period/taper curve on the Predicted 1RM line) is deleted outright — `TrendEngine.predictedOneRepMax` now just takes the best gender-scaled e1RM among the last 3 sessions, full value, no taper.

### "Next Heavy Set" (Lift detail UI name; `TrendEngine.predictNextAtCharacteristicReps`)
Finds the rep count the user actually tends to go heavy at — the **mode of each session's heaviest-set rep count** (by load; bodyweight-adjusted via an optional `loadOf` selector for `ExerciseType.bodyweight` lifts) — then projects a **weight at that same rep count** from recent history at that rep count: last same-rep weight + the average session-to-session delta across up to the last 5 same-rep data points. Confidence band width is `40 / sqrt(n)` lb (clamped 5-40, `n` = same-rep-count sample size) — narrows as more matching history accumulates. **No formula converts between rep ranges anywhere in this calculation** — that's the whole point: a set pushed to true failure at a non-characteristic rep count never gets Epley-inflated into a misleading 1RM estimate, because it's simply excluded from the same-rep history rather than converted. Covered by `trend_engine_test.dart`. Glossary: `next_heavy_set`.

A real newbie stretch (rapid recent gains) and a near-plateau (flat recent deltas) both fall out of the average-delta math on their own — no separate "assume they're new" flag needed, unlike the superseded version below which had explicit newbie-vs-established branching logic.

### Superseded (kept for history, no longer in the app): `predictNextE1rm`'s RPE-weighted blend + gender-scaled rep conversion + growth-rate cap
The prior implementation blended the last session's e1RM with a 5-session average (RPE-weighted), stepped it by a trend-scaled overload percentage (+1.5-3%), applied a gender-scaled Epley divisor (36 female / 30 male) to correct for women's relatively flatter strength-vs-rep-range curve, and capped the step size via a 12-session long-run growth-rate lookback so an established lift wasn't read as still making newbie-style jumps. All of that reasoning (the gender-scaling rationale, the growth-rate-cap rationale) was sound as far as it went, but the entire mechanism still depended on converting between rep ranges via Epley, which is exactly what broke on the real deadlift data. The **gender-scaled Epley divisor is still used** — just narrowed to `TrendEngine.predictedOneRepMax` (the Goal gauge's "Predicted 1RM" line) instead of the near-term box, since that's a genuine 1RM-estimate context where a rep-conversion formula is the right tool. The **muscle-dependent-tuning idea was never implemented** either version — still a real open question, still out of scope.

**Explicitly, this is a "try for this next" progressive-overload target, not a same-day prediction.** It deliberately does NOT factor in today's soreness/sleep/recency — that's `ReadinessEngine`'s job (a separate question: "how do I look today" vs. "what should I aim for over time"). Confirmed with the user this separation is intentional, not a gap.

### Trend chart — reworked to plot raw weight, dot size = reps, trend line shape borrowed from e1RM
The Lift detail "Heavy Set History" chart (`CenteredTrendChart`) used to plot e1RM values. Per the user's ask, it now plots each session's **literal heaviest-set weight** (bodyweight-adjusted where relevant) — no formula — with the **rep count on that set encoded as dot radius** (1 rep = smallest dot, 21+ reps = largest, linear in between), so a chart mixing rep ranges reads as "that point's low because it was a high-rep day," not an unexplained outlier. The dashed trend line still needs *some* comparable-across-rep-counts series to shape it meaningfully (raw weight at varying rep counts isn't directly comparable point-to-point) — solved per the user's own suggestion ("trend of 1RM but shifted to the center"): the polynomial/moving-average fit still runs on gender-scaled e1RM (`ChartPoint.trendValue`), but the resulting curve is shifted by a constant (`mean(raw weights) - mean(e1RM values)`) so it sits centered on the actual plotted data instead of floating off on its own scale. `ChartPoint` gained `reps` and `trendValue` as optional fields (both `null`-default, so every other chart in the app — Home/Metrics bodyweight/steps/sleep — is unaffected).

## Layer 3 — RPE/effort-weighted confidence
Not every set counts equally. A set logged at RPE 9-10 is a strong, high-confidence data point about current max capability; a set at RPE 3-5 (recovery/light day) is a real data point but should pull the trend/estimate less. Concretely: weight each e1RM sample's contribution to the rolling trend by a function of its RPE (near-max effort = high weight, low effort = low weight) rather than by raw recency alone. This directly solves the "I did a 30%-effort day, don't read that as a strength loss" problem the user raised.

**Implemented so far:** `lastIntensity` classifies a session's average RPE into All-out / Normal / Recovery-light, shown on the lift detail screen. (The RPE-weighted blending this paragraph used to describe lived in `predictNextE1rm`, since replaced by `predictNextAtCharacteristicReps` — see Layer 2 above, which does not RPE-weight its blend.) The Lifts-list and Lift-detail readiness scores now both consume the full soreness/RPE/sleep/overload signal via `ReadinessEngine` (see below) — **not yet implemented at the Home Status cards**, which are still the plain-text layer-1 recency flags.

Recovery/readiness flags at this layer combine:
- days since last session for that lift/movement pattern (layer 1)
- recent soreness trend (short 7d rolling average, see glossary re: ACWR-style windows)
- recent RPE trend (climbing RPE for the same weight/reps over time = fatigue accumulating)

Still just weighted arithmetic/exponential smoothing — no training required, but grounded in real strength-training research (RPE-to-%1RM correlation, load-monitoring literature) rather than arbitrary made-up weights. This is the layer where "look over research on soreness/lifting/speed-of-strength-change" work should get folded in as tuning for the weighting functions.

## "Primed for Growth" muscle map — the app's compass (implemented)
The user's framing for this one, verbatim-in-spirit: this map is meant to be **the actual navigation compass for the whole app** — not a coach telling you what to train, just the clearest possible read of where your body is right now, at a glance. It's the first layer-3+ feature that actually consumes soreness/RPE/sleep/overload data, not just recency.

**`ReadinessEngine.computeMuscleReadiness()`** — deliberately computed at **fine-grained muscle resolution** (the same 23-`Muscle` set the lift diagrams and soreness map already use), not just the 5 broad categories. "Squat = legs" is a useful simplification for the Training Composition chart, but this map can do better because we already know exactly which specific muscles each exercise hits (`MuscleMap.musclesFor`) — e.g. a squat's quads and a deadlift's hamstrings can read differently even though both are "legs." Soreness is still only logged at broad-category resolution, so it's applied as a proxy to every muscle within its category — the one place this map is still working from a simplification, until soreness logging itself gets more granular.

Per muscle, factors combine **multiplicatively** into a single 0-1 readiness score:
1. **Recency, normalized by a muscle-specific recovery window** (`_recoveryHours`) — bigger/multi-joint muscle groups (quads, hamstrings, glutes, lower back: 72h) get longer windows than smaller/isolated ones (biceps, triceps, abs: 24h), a directional generalization from standard strength-training recovery guidance, not a number measured for this specific user. Never trained = fully recovered (1.0). **Now age-adjusted** — every window is scaled by `UserProfile.ageBucket.recoveryWindowMultiplier` (`user_profile.dart`) before use: 0.9× for under-20, 1.0× (reference) for 20s, climbing to 1.5× for 60+. Opposite direction from the strength-standard age adjustment on purpose — recovery from muscle damage genuinely slows with age even as expected max strength also declines; two different curves sharing one bucket, not one.
2. **Soreness** (sub-region proxy, 0-5) — pulls readiness down as soreness rises (`1 - (level/5)*0.6`, so max soreness caps the penalty at 60%, doesn't zero it out entirely). **Now decays over time when nothing new is logged (2026-07-22)** — see `05_SCREEN_metrics.md` "Soreness decay" for the full writeup; previously a stale log from arbitrarily far back was treated as still 100% current forever, which this fixes via `MetricsRepository.effectiveSorenessLevel`.
3. **Sleep** (global, not per-muscle, `ReadinessEngine.sleepFactorFor` — **changed 2026-07-21, was last-3-nights' average**) — cumulative deficit over the last 7 nights against a 7.5h reference, clamped to a 0.7-1.0 multiplier. Poor recent sleep dents readiness everywhere, not just one region. **Why cumulative-deficit over a 7-night window instead of a plain 3-night average:** this is the standard framing in sleep-debt research (e.g. Van Dongen et al. on cumulative sleep restriction) — deficit accumulates across short nights and isn't meaningfully offset by one good night mixed in among bad ones, which a plain average would wash out. Concretely: `totalDeficit = sum(max(0, 7.5 - night))` across up to 7 nights (a night above the reference clamps to zero deficit rather than banking credit against a prior bad night), then `factor = clamp(1 - avgNightlyDeficit * 0.3, 0.7, 1.0)`. A sustained ~1h/night average deficit lands right at the same 0.7 floor the old flat average used — same floor, steadier week-long signal reaching it instead of 3 noisy nights. Pure logic lives in `sleepFactorFor` (unit-tested, `readiness_engine_test.dart`) for the same reason `volumeSpikeDetected` is split out — testable without a DB harness.
4. **Performance trend, as a stretched recovery window rather than an instant penalty** (`ReadinessEngine.recentWorkoutPerformance` — **redesigned 2026-07-21**, replacing the old separate RPE-trend/overload-trend multiplicative factors). If the most recent workout hitting a muscle reads as genuinely worse than normal (RPE notably higher than usual, or e1RM down >5% against its own recent average), that muscle's recovery *window itself* gets multiplied by `poorPerformanceWindowStretch` (1.3×) before recency is measured against it — performance sets the starting point, time-based recovery is what brings it back to neutral either way. A normal/good workout leaves the window untouched (identical to the pre-redesign behavior). See "Cross-exercise pooling bug — fixed" below for what actually changed and why.
5. **Session volume spike** (`ReadinessEngine.volumeSpikeDetected`) — if the most recent session hitting a muscle moved notably more total weight (reps × weight, **same exercise only**) than that exercise's own recent average, that session earns extra recovery credit beyond pure recency (factor 0.9). Added because the model previously had no concept of "how much work did you actually do" at all — only whether RPE/e1RM happened to trend, which says nothing about a session that was simply bigger than usual at a consistent RPE. Was always scoped to the **same exercise** (never had the pooling bug factor 4 used to have, below) — this one was written to dodge that mistake from the start. Gated behind `minSessionsForTrendSignal`, same as factor 4. Covered by `readiness_engine_test.dart`.
6. **Extra lower-body load from steps or logged cardio** — legs/hips muscles only (`MuscleMap.broadGroups[ExerciseCategory.legs]`), from either signal coming in well above the user's own recent norm (factor 0.85 when triggered). Grounded in real research on the "interference effect" between endurance activity and strength/hypertrophy recovery — a big walk/run day taxes the same lower-body muscles endurance work actually uses, on top of whatever lifting already happened. Deliberately scoped to leg/hip muscles only, not applied globally — walking doesn't meaningfully tax chest/arms. Compared against the user's own rolling average (same self-relative pattern as every other trend factor here), not a fixed step target. **Named modestly on purpose**: this is framed as "extra physical load," not "CNS fatigue" — true CNS/nervous-system fatigue shows up in things like grip strength, reaction time, or HRV, none of which this app measures, so claiming to detect it from step count alone would be overclaiming. **Placeholder for Google Health Connect**: once that integration exists, HRV and resting heart rate would be far more direct systemic-fatigue signals than steps and would slot in the same way (an extra self-relative multiplicative factor) — noted as a comment directly above `ReadinessEngine._stepsLoadFactor` so the hook is easy to find later. Not implemented, no dead code added — just a comment marking where it goes.
   - **Added 2026-07-21: `ReadinessEngine.cardioLoadSpikeDetected`, a second, more specific proxy for the same interference effect.** Raw step count is a decent but coarse signal — a logged cardio session (`cardio_sessions`/`cardio_entries`, already tracked: duration, and often RPE) is a much more direct one. Compares the most recent cardio session's `duration_seconds × (avgRpe / 6.0)` (an un-rated session defaults intensity to 1.0 off a "moderate effort" reference, rather than being skipped) against the average of up to 5 prior sessions, flagging a spike at >1.3× — same ratio-vs-own-history shape as `volumeSpikeDetected`. Two gates, mirroring the other trend signals: the spike session must be within the last 48h (the shortest muscle recovery window in `_recoveryHours`, so a hard run from a week ago doesn't still count as loading today's legs), and there must be at least `minSessionsForTrendSignal - 1` prior sessions to compare against (same newbie-swings reasoning as the grace period below). **Steps and cardio are two overlapping proxies for the same real-world event** (a big run shows up in both) — `computeMuscleReadiness` combines them with `min`, not by multiplying, so one hard run session doesn't get double-penalized for tripping both signals at once. Pure logic in `cardioLoadSpikeDetected` is unit-tested (`readiness_engine_test.dart`); `_cardioLoadFactor` is the thin AppServices-fetching wrapper, same split as every other factor here. **Deliberately HIIT-free for now** — a HIIT session's load doesn't reduce to one comparable number as cleanly as cardio's duration/RPE (mixed reps/weight/time/distance slots per round), a real modeling question rather than a quick addition; noted as the natural next extension, not implemented.

**Grace period before trend signals apply — added.** Factor 4 only evaluates once a muscle/exercise has **`ReadinessEngine.minSessionsForTrendSignal` (4) sessions** of logged history — below that, it has no effect (window unstretched), same as if nothing had happened yet. Added after a new user reported everything reading as "overreaching" almost immediately. Root cause: with only `sessions.length >= 2` required, the very first session was being compared against a "baseline" of exactly one prior session — for a brand-new lifter (or a lift they'd just started), normal early session-to-session swings (still learning the movement, still calibrating what weight/RPE actually feels like) got read as a fatigue trend. This is a well-documented distinct phase in strength-training literature — early sessions are dominated by rapid neural/technique-learning adaptation ("newbie gains"), not accumulating fatigue, and shouldn't be judged against a fatigue-trend model built for an established baseline. Requiring 4 sessions also fixes a second, independent issue: it guarantees the "prior" baseline being compared against is an average of up to 3 sessions, not a single noisy one.

**Cross-exercise pooling bug — fixed 2026-07-21.** Previously documented here as "known, not yet fixed": `sessionsByMuscle`/`sessionsByCategory` pool every session from *any* exercise that hits a given muscle/category — e.g. Back Squat, Leg Press, and Hip Adduction Machine all feed into `Muscle.quadriceps`'s session list. The old `trendFatigueDetected` compared one exercise's raw e1RM/RPE against an average pooled across *different* exercises sharing the tag — a Hip Adduction Machine session (e.g. ~60lb e1RM) logged near a Squat-heavy stretch (e.g. ~300lb e1RM) could mask a real dip or manufacture a fake one, depending entirely on which exercise happened to sort first and what else was in the pooled "prior" window. Found via a real case: a user sick for several days had their last squat/deadlift session crater hard (an actual ~35% e1RM drop), but the Legs status icon read "Ready" while Back and Arms read "Overreaching" — traced to Deadlift's `back,legs,pull` category tags spreading the same event across buckets of very different pool composition and size.

Fixed with `ReadinessEngine.recentWorkoutPerformance`, now shared by both `computeMuscleReadiness` and `computeCategoryStatus` (previously each had its own copy of the buggy logic) — full doc comment on the function itself, short version: "today's workout" is every session sharing the most recent date (not just whichever one happened to sort first), each exercise is compared **only against its own prior sessions** (never another exercise's absolute numbers), and same-day exercises are combined by a **tonnage-weighted average** rather than picking one — a heavy primary lift and a light cooldown/accessory sharing the tag reads as "normal" instead of "overtrained" just because the cooldown alone looks light, per the user's own framing of the fix. Weighting by each exercise's own logged tonnage means this self-adjusts to how the user actually trains (a couple of heavy compounds vs. many lighter accessory lifts) with no separate calibration rule needed for either style. Verified against the user's own reference-data export (`reference-data/terpinlift_export(1).json`) during design — confirmed the real data really does mix barbell lifts and machine accessories under the same category tags, and hand-traced the new weighted-ratio math against those real numbers before writing it.

**Unified with the status icons, and "overreaching" redefined.** Previously the heatmap (continuous 0-1 score) and the status icons (`computeCategoryStatus`) each decided "was this workout bad" independently, using two separate copies of the same buggy logic — a second bug distinct from the pooling one, and the direct cause of the heatmap/icons disagreement in the case above (the heatmap's dominant recency factor pushed the score back up since a few days had passed, while the icons' independent slump check fired on a different, differently-pooled bucket). Both now call the exact same `recentWorkoutPerformance` verdict. Per the user's own framing — "performance based as the starting point, then time base to bring them back to neutral" — a poor verdict no longer applies an instant separate penalty; it stretches that muscle/category's recovery window by `poorPerformanceWindowStretch`, and *time* is what brings it back to "ready" either way, just more slowly after a bad workout. This means "Overreaching" in the status icons now specifically means "still inside the *extra*, stretched portion of recovery because the last workout read as poor" rather than an independent instant flag — once `hoursSince` clears the stretched window, status is "Ready" regardless of history, matching "eventually it evens out."

**`poorPerformanceWindowStretch` (1.3×) is a rough starting heuristic, not a research-backed figure — flagged for revisiting.** Asked directly whether there's solid research tying a specific performance-drop percentage to a specific extra-recovery-hours number: there isn't a clean one. Functional overreaching is described in the literature as resolving over "days to weeks" in general terms, not a formula. The user's own call, given that: a flat, capped multiplier (not one that scales continuously with how bad the dip was) specifically to avoid a data glitch or one unusually light warm-up set producing an extreme stretch. Revisit this number once real usage shows whether it feels right.

**Volume-touched-lightly gap — narrowed, not fully closed.** The map is still a *recovery-state* signal, not a *training-completeness* one — factor 5 (session volume spike, above) only catches a session that was **bigger than usual**, it doesn't distinguish "3 token sit-ups" from "a genuinely light but intentional session" the way a real completeness signal would. A very lightly touched muscle can still read fairly ready once its (now age-adjusted) recovery window elapses. Considered acceptable for now — the alternative (defining what counts as "enough" training) is a much bigger, more opinionated modeling decision than this app wants to make, closer to coaching than guiding.

**Color display — fixed.** The color ramp used to be a plain 2-stop linear lerp (`BodyHeatmap`'s `colors: [surfaceRaised, AppColors.good]`) with no amber/warning midpoint — confirmed by reading the package source (`_BodyHeatmapPainter`, intensity `t.clamp(0,1)` lerped directly across those two colors, no relative/min-max normalization anywhere in the render path). Green is also the most perceptually salient RGB channel, so even a moderate score (say 0.5) rendered fairly vividly green, compressing the visual gap between "somewhat ready" and "fully ready" — confirmed by walking through a real example (chest ~26-30h after a heavy bench session: `recoveryFraction` alone computes to ~0.43-0.5 given chest's 60h window, i.e. genuinely "moderate," but displayed as "lit up, good to go"). Replaced with `ReadinessBands` (`lib/widgets/readiness_bands.dart`) — four hand-picked, explicitly named colors (`AppColors.readinessNeedsRest/Recovering/Ready/WellRested`) stepped at fixed thresholds (0.3 / 0.55 / 0.8), passed via `MuscleData(color: ...)` (bypassing the widget's own continuous lerp entirely, since `MuscleData.color` takes priority over `intensity`) rather than a smoother gradient — deliberately discrete/legible bands over continuous math, matching how the rest of the app already represents readiness (`ReadinessBars`' 0-5 discrete bars, `MuscleGroupStatus`'s 3-state icons) rather than inventing a second continuous scale. `ReadinessBands.labelFor` gives each band a plain word too ("Needs rest" / "Recovering" / "Ready" / "Well rested"), available for reuse anywhere a short description is more useful than a raw number.

**Temporary on-device calibration widget** — `ReadinessCalibrationBar` (`lib/widgets/readiness_calibration_bar.dart`), a small legend strip showing all four bands' colors/thresholds/labels side by side, added directly below the Home heatmap purely so the user can eyeball the new bands against the old feel. **Meant to be deleted** (the widget file and its one call site in `home_screen.dart`) once the bands are confirmed to look right — not a permanent fixture, don't build on top of it.

**Visual:** front + back small heatmaps on Home, colored on a **green scale**, deliberately a different hue family from the soreness map's red — the two widgets look similar (same package, same body silhouette) but must never be visually confused for meaning the same thing (soreness = fatigue/bad, this map = ready/good).

**Revised — the compass is now the single source of truth for readiness everywhere it appears.** Originally shipped additively alongside the recency-only Lifts-list readiness bars without changing them; the next round unified them: `ReadinessEngine.readinessForExercise(exercise, muscleReadiness)` averages the per-muscle scores across whatever muscles an exercise hits (`MuscleMap.musclesFor`) into one 0-1 number per lift, and `ReadinessEngine.toBars` maps that onto the existing 0-5 bar scale — same visual language (`ReadinessBars`), richer number underneath. Both the Lifts list and the Lift-detail readiness card read from this. **Now fully unified**: the Home Status cards (previously the plain-text layer-1 recency flags, the one piece that had never been revisited) are replaced by the category-status icon row below.

**In-app explanation, deliberately incomplete:** the muscle map has an "i" info icon (`Glossary._entries['readiness']`) that names every contributing factor and gestures at the muscle-size-dependent-recovery research direction, but does not give the recovery-hour table or the exact multiplier values — enough for "that makes sense, I trust it," not enough to reverse-engineer. See `08_GLOSSARY_AND_SCIENCE.md`.

### Muscle status icons (implemented — replaces the old text Status cards)
`ReadinessEngine.computeCategoryStatus()` — a **per-category** (the same 5 broad `TrainingCompositionService.bodyPartCategories`: legs/back/chest/core/arms) verdict, one of `MuscleGroupStatus.ready` / `.resting` / `.overreaching`. Deliberately recomputed at category granularity rather than derived from `computeMuscleReadiness`'s output, since that function only returns the final multiplied 0-1 score — it doesn't preserve *which* factor drove it, and telling "just resting" apart from "actively overreaching" needs that distinction.

- **Priority per category**: overreaching beats resting beats ready.
  - **Overreaching**: `ReadinessEngine.trendFatigueDetected` (RPE creeping up >1.5 vs. the prior few sessions, or e1RM down >5% against its own recent average, both gated behind a 4-session grace period — see above) — the same shared function `computeMuscleReadiness`'s RPE-trend/overload-trend factors use, computed directly per category instead of being blended away into one multiplied number.
  - **Naming note, since the user asked to double-check this against the real literature**: what this flags is *functional overreaching* — a short-term RPE/performance dip, the kind that's expected to resolve within days-to-weeks by easing off, and a completely normal part of training. It is **not** Overtraining Syndrome (OTS) — a much rarer, clinically-defined condition involving hormonal/immune disruption and performance decrements lasting 2+ months, which this app makes no attempt to detect (correctly so — that's a medical concern, not a training-log heuristic). The `overreaching` enum name and the tooltip copy ("this one's effort has been creeping up... ease up on this group for a bit," `MuscleStatusRow._explanationFor`) were already scoped correctly to the lighter concept; the actual bug was the grace period above, not the terminology.
  - **Resting**: hours since that category was last trained are still inside its typical recovery window (averaged across the category's member muscles' `_recoveryHours`).
  - **Ready**: recovery window elapsed, or never trained (matches the "never trained = fully ready" convention used elsewhere).
- **Deliberately per-category, not one global verdict** — direct from the user's framing: "I don't need a rest day even if my legs are burnt, I can go do arms." A blanket status would force a false choice between ignoring real fatigue and blocking unrelated training.
- **`MuscleStatusRow`** (`02_SCREEN_home.md`): one icon per category — shape carries which status (bed = resting, weight = ready, warning-triangle = overreaching), color carries urgency (resting is muted grey since it's the normal/expected state and not something to act on; ready is green; overreaching is amber). Tapping a chip shows a one-line plain-language explanation for that specific category.
- **Independent signal from the readiness map/primed-lifts row, by design — but the user is right that this raises a real "does it all line up" question.** `computeCategoryStatus` is recency + RPE/overload-trend based; the muscle heatmap and `suggestPrimedLifts` are both driven by the fine-grained `computeMuscleReadiness` scores instead. They *can* legitimately disagree since they model different things, but the user's own read after using it — icons and the map "mostly" agreeing, an occasional lift suggestion (Clean and Jerk, a whole-body movement) not obviously matching a mixed-readiness picture — pointed at a real scoring bug in the suggestion algorithm (fixed below), not a fundamental icon problem. **Not yet decided**: whether `computeCategoryStatus` should be unified onto the same `computeMuscleReadiness` scores for full consistency, or kept as an intentionally separate recency-based signal — the user is still forming an opinion on what they actually want from the icons (leaning toward wanting something more holistic/navigational on top, not a replacement) before committing either way.

### Primed-lift suggestions (implemented, scoring revised)
`ReadinessEngine.suggestPrimedLifts(exercises, muscleReadiness, {maxLifts: 5, primedThreshold: 0.6})` — a **greedy weighted set-cover**, not a ranked list: repeatedly picks whichever remaining exercise adds the most *additional* coverage of currently-primed muscles (readiness ≥ 0.6), then removes those muscles from further consideration before picking again. This is exactly what makes it non-redundant — per the user's example, if some leg muscles are primed, Front Squat and Back Squat overlap heavily (both hit primed quads), so the second one only earns a slot if it covers primed muscles the first one missed (e.g. Front Squat's core emphasis vs. Back Squat's posterior-chain lean), rather than both appearing just because both happen to be "leg lifts." Stops early if no remaining exercise adds any new coverage, even before hitting the 5-lift cap. Surfaced as `PrimedLiftsRow` directly under the Primed for Growth map (`02_SCREEN_home.md`) — a guide ("here's what covers what's primed"), never a "you should do X" prescription.

**Bug found via the user's own alignment check: per-pick scoring was SUM, not average, of newly-covered readiness — fixed.** A lift touching many muscles that are each only barely over the 0.6 threshold (e.g. Clean and Jerk brushing several moderately-primed muscles across legs/back/arms) could out-score a lift touching one or two muscles that are genuinely well-primed, just because the total added up higher — exactly the "map shows mostly yellow, but a whole-body lift got suggested" mismatch the user flagged. Now scores each candidate by the **average** readiness of the muscles it would newly cover (ties broken by covering more muscles), so a solidly-primed narrow pick (e.g. Chest Press Machine when chest is clearly green) outranks a broadly-but-marginally-primed one. Covered by `readiness_engine_test.dart`.

## Strength-standard Goal system (implemented — main goal only, sub-goals dropped)
**2026-07-14 concern resolved as: custom goals.** The user's earlier "revisit later, no specifics yet" flag turned out to be about wanting a way to set/switch to her own target — implemented 2026-07-15, see "Custom goals" below.

A **separate, longer-arc** target system from the "Next Heavy Set" box above — deliberately not merged into that box. The distinction, in the user's own framing: the prediction box is "try for this next" (near-term, projected from recent sessions at a characteristic rep count); the Goal gauge is "here's a realistic long-term target for this lift, given your bodyweight" (bodyweight-ratio strength standards, gender + age adjusted, **or the user's own custom number**).

### Custom goals (implemented 2026-07-15, reworked into a log the same day)
Lets the user set their own target for any exercise — including ones that *do* have a published standard, per her explicit ask ("the user should be able to add and switch to a user-made goal list if they prefer, for lifts that do have a real published table behind it"), not just as a fallback for exercises with none.

**Reworked same-day, before ever shipping as a single value**: the user's follow-up ask was "somethign more like the lift log as an entry... put in (or remove) multiples and also give them some text... this way the user can see old goals and can name them." A single overwritable target didn't match that — goals are now a **log**, same spirit as a lift session log, not a settings field.

- **`CustomGoal`** (`data/models/custom_goal.dart`) + `custom_goals` table (DB v13 — the v12 shape, one-per-exercise via a UNIQUE constraint, is dropped and recreated rather than migrated, since it shipped without any real usage yet): many rows per exercise, each with an optional free-text `label`, a `created` timestamp, and either `target_weight` (lb, canonical) or `target_reps`. The **most recent entry (by `created`) drives the gauge**; everything older is just browsable history — no separate "current vs. archived" flag needed, recency already encodes that.
- **`CustomGoalHistorySheet`** (`screens/lifts/custom_goal_history_sheet.dart`) — opened via the "tune" icon next to the Goal header. A small add-entry row (optional label + a value field matching the lift's axis, "+" button) at the top, and every existing entry listed below (newest first) showing its value, its label (or its date if unlabeled), and a delete icon. Adds/deletes persist immediately, no separate save step — same as adding a set in the lift-log form.
- **`Exercise.goalSource`** (`GoalSource.standard`/`.custom`, `null` = auto): `null` picks a standard if one exists, else the latest custom-goal entry if one exists, else no goal shown. Explicit `.standard`/`.custom` lets the user override — e.g. force a custom number even on Back Squat, which has a published standard. Lift detail shows a small "Standard"/"My Goal" chip toggle **only when both exist** for that lift (nothing to toggle otherwise); tapping persists via `Exercise.copyWith(goalSource:...)`.
- **Rendering**: `SingleGoalGauge` (`widgets/single_goal_gauge.dart`) — a lightweight sibling to `StrengthGoalGauge`, same track/fill visual language and the same optional Predicted 1RM line below (see "e1RM confusion" below). **2026-07-16 fix: plots every logged goal, not just the newest.** The first version only ever showed the latest entry as a single tick — the user caught this immediately ("it did not carry over to the progress bar... it should say the name of the goal under it, rn it just seems to show the largest goal"), since the whole point of making this a log was to keep old goals visible. Now takes a `List<GoalMarker>` (`{value, label}`), one tick per matching log entry (sorted ascending), domain scaled to the largest goal (same idea as the standard gauge scaling to Elite) — each tick labeled with its value above and its own name (or its date, if unlabeled) below, and the highest goal actually reached bolded/accented the same way the standard gauge highlights the current tier. The progress bar's fill is **always** the real true max/best rep count (never a prediction, never any custom target) — same non-negotiable as the standard gauge, confirmed explicitly unnecessary to change: "if the user's heaviest set ever had 4 reps at 100lbs the progress bar is only filled to 100lbs, no prediction/scaling used in any case."
- **`ProgressMetric` enum exists in the model** (`Exercise.progressMetric`, predicted vs. true-max) but ended up **not wired to any UI toggle** — once the 3 Lift-detail sections were redesigned per the user's spec, each one has an unambiguous, fixed behavior (Next Heavy Set is always a same-rep-count fact-based projection, the trend chart is always raw logged weight, the Goal gauge always shows both True Max and Predicted 1RM together rather than picking one to lead) — there was no longer a live decision left for a "which number leads" toggle to control. Left in the schema/model in case a real use for it turns up, but no dead UI was built around it.

### "Where's the e1RM view?" — resolved as a visibility gap, not a missing toggle
After the redesign above, the user asked how to switch from a "true" view to an "e1RM" view, since Next Heavy Set, the trend chart, and the gauge's fill/True-max all read as fact-based now. Asked directly whether she wanted a toggle changing what drives the gauge's fill (predicted vs. true) or just better visibility of the e1RM number that already exists — she confirmed the latter: **Predicted 1RM was already there**, just easy to miss as a small secondary line. Real gap found and fixed in the process: `SingleGoalGauge` (the custom-goal path) never had a `predictedValue` param at all, so switching a lift's Goal source to "My Goal" silently hid the Predicted 1RM line entirely — now takes the same `predictedValue` param as `StrengthGoalGauge` and shows the same "Predicted 1RM: [value]" line regardless of goal source. No new toggle built, since there wasn't actually a live decision left to toggle.

### Searchable exercise picker (implemented 2026-07-15)
The exercise-switch dropdown on `EditLiftSessionForm` (added earlier this session) and the exercise picker on `LogLiftForm`/quick-log both used to be pinned-lifts-only dropdowns — fast for the common case, but couldn't reach an unpinned exercise without leaving the form. Per the user's ask for "a clean middle ground," both are now `ExercisePickerField` (`widgets/exercise_picker_field.dart`, built on Flutter's `Autocomplete`): empty/untouched shows just the pinned (+ current/preselected) list, same as before; typing searches every exercise in the library by name, pinned or not. Shows what matters first, lets you search for the rest — no separate "show all" toggle needed.

- **`StrengthStandards`**: 5 tiers (Beginner/Novice/Intermediate/Advanced/Elite, matching how most published strength-standard aggregators like Strength Level/ExRx structure their tables) × bodyweight multiples × gender, for the 4 lifts with commonly-cited standards (Back Squat, Bench Press, Deadlift, Overhead Press). Front Squat has no separately-published standard — derived as Back Squat × 0.83 (a commonly cited rough ratio, not independently sourced). Custom exercises get no goal (nothing to map them to).
- **Age handled as buckets, not an exact age** — per the user's explicit call: a 21-year-old and a 56-year-old have very different realistic expectations, but a smooth age-graduated formula is more precision than this needs. `AgeBucket` (under-20/20s/30s/40s/50s/60+) each carry a multiplier applied on top of the base (20s-reference) standard — roughly flat through the 20s, gradual decline through the 30s-40s, steeper after 50, loosely mirroring the shape of published age-graduated powerlifting adjustment formulas without claiming that level of precision.
- **New Settings fields**: Gender (default Female) and Birth year (converted to `AgeBucket`, unset defaults to the 20s/no-adjustment bucket) — both live in `06_SCREEN_settings.md`, both used only by this feature so far.
- **`StrengthGoalGauge`**: horizontal progress bar with 5 tick marks at each tier threshold **with the target weight labeled above each tick and the tier abbreviation below** (e.g. `75LB` / tick / `Nov`). Tick weights recompute automatically as bodyweight/gender/age-bucket change, since `tierTargets` is derived fresh from the latest values on every screen load.
- **True max vs. Predicted 1RM — two separate numbers.** The gauge's fill/tier position and its "True max: [weight]" label are the highest `LiftSet.weight` ever logged for this exercise (`LiftDetailScreen`'s `trueMaxSet`/`trueMaxWeight`), full history, no Epley projection, no decay — a PR is a historical fact, it doesn't get inflated by rep math and doesn't decay just because you haven't trained the lift lately. **As of 2026-07-15, this label also shows the rep count that max was achieved at** ("True max: 100lb, 4 reps") — the user's ask, so the number reads as what it actually was rather than implying a clean single. **Predicted 1RM** (`TrendEngine.predictedOneRepMax` — gender-scaled Epley off the best of the last 3 sessions, see Layer 2 above) is a second, clearly-separate line below "True max," with its own "i" tooltip (`predicted_1rm` glossary entry) explicitly stating it's an estimate, not something actually lifted. `StrengthGoalGauge`/`SingleGoalGauge` both take an optional `predictedValue`/`currentReps` param for this — `null` skips the line (used for the bodyweight-reps variant below, where "predicted" isn't as meaningful a concept).
- **Decay removed entirely (2026-07-15).** `StrengthStandards.effectiveBestE1rm` (the taper curve previously applied to the Predicted 1RM number) is deleted — see Layer 2 above for why. The ideas below were about *that* decay mechanism specifically and no longer apply now that the mechanism itself is gone; kept here as history of what was considered, not as live open questions.
  - *(Historical, no longer applicable)* a consistency-weighted "confidence" version of the current PR, cross-lift/muscle-group decay dampening, and age/gender-adjusted decay rates were all considered and not built, back when decay still existed.
- **Dropped, not deferred**: personalized short-term sub-goals ("safe next PR" tailored to the user's own recent growth rate, sitting between current PR and the main goal). Talking it through, this turned out to just be a re-skin of the near-term prediction box — same inputs (recent sessions, progressive overload), same near-term job. Two numbers both claiming to answer "what should you hit next" would invite "which do I trust" rather than add information, so the plan is to keep exactly two numbers on this screen: the Next Heavy Set box (near-term, bottom-up from recent performance) and the Goal gauge (long-arc, top-down from bodyweight standards or a custom target) — not three.
- **Same directional-generalization caveat as everything else in this doc**: the "i" tooltip (`strength_goal` glossary entry) states plainly these are commonly-cited-style approximate figures, not a personally measured standard.

### Bodyweight movements (Pull Up, Push Up) — implemented
`ExerciseType.bodyweight`-tagged lifts (see `03_SCREEN_lifts.md` equipment tags) don't fit the barbell model — `LiftSet.weight` for these is the **signed added/assisted load** relative to bodyweight (negative = assisted, 0 = plain bodyweight, positive = weighted), entered exactly as typed (e.g. `-40`, `0`, `+25`), not a total. Two real problems this created, and how each was solved:
- **Epley breaks at weight <= 0.** `e1RM = weight × (1 + reps/30)` is always 0 at plain bodyweight and goes negative when assisted — nonsensical as a "max" estimate. Fixed with `SessionWithSets.bodyweightAdjustedBestE1rm(bodyweightLb)`, which feeds Epley the **total load actually moved** (`bodyweightLb + addedLoad`) instead — always positive, monotonic across the whole assisted → bodyweight → weighted range. `TrendEngine.predictNextAtCharacteristicReps`'s optional `loadOf` selector (and `predictedOneRepMax`'s `e1rmValueOf`) take this same total-load function so the substitution is a call-site swap in `LiftDetailScreen`, not a rewrite of the prediction math.
- **2026-07-15: the near-term box is no longer reps-shaped at all, even for bodyweight lifts.** It used to relabel to "Predicted Next Rep Range" and invert Epley back to a rep count at a reference load. Now that the near-term prediction (`predictNextAtCharacteristicReps`) works by finding a characteristic *rep count* and projecting a *weight* at that fixed rep count (see Layer 2 above), it's already generically correct for bodyweight lifts once fed the bodyweight-adjusted `loadOf` — no separate reps-unit branch needed. The box always shows a weight (total load — bodyweight + added, for these lifts), never reps, across every exercise type.
  - **Goal gauge** still switches its tier source to `BodyweightRepStandards` (a smaller, rep-count-shaped table — Pull Up/Push Up × gender × tier, no bodyweight input needed) instead of `StrengthStandards`, and its current-position marker is pinned to the **best plain-bodyweight rep count only** (`weight == 0` sets) — a weighted or assisted PR isn't comparable to published unassisted-rep standards, so it's deliberately excluded from this specific number (it still counts fully toward the Next Heavy Set box above). **No longer decayed** (decay removed entirely, see above) — `prBestReps` is now just the raw best rep count, a historical fact like the barbell True Max. This rep count is **not** split into a true-max/predicted pair the way the barbell gauge is — the gauge's `predictedValue` param is left `null` here, since "predicted 1RM" isn't a meaningful concept for a rep-count goal.
  - Both `RangeIndicator` and `StrengthGoalGauge` gained an optional `formatValue` override (default: unit-aware lb formatting) so this is purely a display-layer swap to `'$reps reps'`, not a fork of either widget.
- **`BodyweightRepStandards`** table (approximate, same directional-generalization caveat as everything else — see `strength_goal_bodyweight` glossary entry): Pull Up male 1/4/8/14/20, female 1/2/4/8/12; Push Up male 5/12/20/35/50, female 3/8/15/25/40 (Beginner→Elite). Age-bucket multiplier reused from `AgeBucket.standardMultiplier`, rounded to a whole rep, floored at 1.
- **Seeded exercises**: Pull Up (back/pull/arms) and Push Up (chest/push/arms), both tagged `bodyweight`, added to the default 5-lift seed list and to `TestDataService`'s synthetic-data narrative — Pull Up walks the full assisted (-55→-30lb) → plain-bodyweight (4→12 reps) → weighted (+5→+25lb) arc over the 60-day demo window; Push Up stays plain-bodyweight the whole window, reps climbing 8→32, so both the three-phase transition and a pure rep-climb are visible via Settings → Load Test Data.

## Test data now has real narrative shape (matters for testing layers 3-4 later)
`TestDataService` was deliberately made non-trivial rather than a smooth monotonic ramp, specifically so future layer-3/4 work has something realistic to be tested against:
- **Growth curve (`_growthCurve`)**: fast early gains → a plateau → a genuine slump/regression (simulated overreaching, not just noise) → a rebound past the pre-slump level. This gives the adaptive polynomial trend fit (see `00_UX_DESIGN.md`) actual shape to find, rather than always fitting a near-straight line.
- **DOMS-style soreness decay**: a training day's soreness contribution is weighted by days-since-trained (0.4x same day, 1.0x peak the day after, 0.5x two days after, 0 beyond) rather than being a flat same-day bump — matches real delayed-onset-muscle-soreness timing (see `08_GLOSSARY_AND_SCIENCE.md`), and is exactly the kind of signal layer 3's soreness-trend input will eventually read.
- **Cycle-linked dips**: period days get a modest sleep/step reduction and a higher soreness baseline — not a claim about how cycles work, just correlated synthetic noise so the (not-yet-built) cycle-trend flag in this doc has something plausible to eventually find.

## Recomposition flag (weight trend × strength trend)
Separate small heuristic, same "flag, don't fabricate precision" philosophy as everything else:
- Compare bodyweight rolling trend (28d) against e1RM rolling trend (28d) across major lifts.
- If bodyweight is flat/slightly up while e1RM is meaningfully up → surface a qualitative annotation on the weight trend view (something like "consistent with recomposition, not a stall") rather than any invented muscle-mass-gained number.
- Reference ranges for plausible lean-mass-gain rates (training-age-dependent, from commonly cited lean-gain literature — see glossary) inform what counts as "meaningful" e1RM movement for this flag, but the output stays qualitative.

## Cycle-trend flag (separate, its own light heuristic)
Once enough cycle_log + soreness/energy data exists: look for correlation between cycle phase and logged soreness/energy dips (not for "cycle syncing" prescriptions — just a "heads up, you tend to feel low around this point, that's probably why today's numbers are off" flag). Same rule-based-first approach as everything above; stays entirely within the nondescript/private Cycle surface, never bleeds into main Home cards.
